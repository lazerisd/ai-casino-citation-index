Method

Five questions were put to each engine for each jurisdiction, phrased the way a person actually types them rather than as search keywords — for example, “I live in Ontario. Where can I safely play online slots for real money?” Every source the engine reported was recorded, along with the operators it named in the answer text.

Licensed-operator lists were pulled directly from the regulators: the iGaming Ontario operator list, the German GGL whitelist and the New Jersey DGE authorised-sites page. Operator names were matched against the registrable root of each licensed domain — so nj.betmgm.com resolves to betmgm — with generic words such as casino, play and slots excluded to avoid false matches.

Limitations, stated plainly

  • This is a first edition built on 80 answers. It is large enough to show structure, not to support precise percentages. Treat every figure as indicative.
  • Both engines answered all forty of their questions, so the two are evenly weighted. Google's Gemini was excluded: its search grounding is unavailable on the free tier for new accounts, and an ungrounded answer reports no sources at all.
  • All eight registers were read, but not in the same form. Ontario, Germany, Malta, the Netherlands, New Jersey and Pennsylvania publish licensed domains. Denmark publishes licensed legal entities, so its brands had to be inferred from company names, and the UK register has no downloadable list at all and was queried one brand at a time.
  • Because of that, a brand shown as unlicensed in Denmark may simply be a brand whose company name does not reveal it. Read the Danish column as weaker evidence than the others.
  • Name matching is literal. Where a regulator lists a market-specific domain — New Jersey's pokerstarsnj.com is the clearest case — the brand will not match and shows as unlisted. That is a flaw in our method, not a finding about the operator.
  • Source classification into affiliate, sports, news, regulator and trade press was done by hand and is a judgement call at the margins.
  • Answers vary between runs. A single reading is a snapshot, not a ranking.
